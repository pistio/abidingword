## Going to a passage

There are three easy ways:

| Way | How |
|---|---|
| **Type it** | In **Go to passage**, type `John 15`, `Rom 8:1-17` or `Ps 23` and press **Enter** (or click **Go**). |
| **Choose it** | Pick a **book** from the list, then a **chapter**. |
| **Step through** | Use **‹** and **›** beside *Chapter* to go to the previous or next chapter. |

Short names work too: `Jn 3:16`, `1 Cor 13`, `Gen 1`.

When a passage is part of what you are studying, a thin coloured line runs
beside those verses, and the heading says *"showing John 15:1–11"*.

## Going back

The **← →** arrows near the top of the pane work like the back and forward
buttons in a web browser. They take you through the places you have just
read.

## Switching translations

Use the small **translation menu** at the top of the pane (it shows **WEB** in
the picture). Your place is kept: John 15 stays John 15.

> [!NOTE]
> Your highlights and notes belong to the **verse**, not to one translation.
> Switch translation and they are still there.

### A Bible read as a document

A Bible added as a document (see
[Getting started](01-getting-started.md#a-bible-in-a-pdf-epub-or-word-document))
is also in the translation menu. Choosing it shows the document in the pane:
**Original** shows it as it is laid out (a PDF page by page, an EPUB or Word
document with its formatting), **Text** shows its text. Use the search box
above it to find words in it. Abiding Word remembers where you were.

Going to a passage, Scripture search and the concordance use Bibles read by
verse; **Back to …** returns to the one you were reading, at the same place.
**What can I do with this Bible?** explains this, and offers **Look for
verses…**: Abiding Word reads the document again for books, chapters and
verses, shows you what it found, and — once you confirm — the Bible can be
read by book, chapter and verse.

If only some chapters are clear, Abiding Word offers **Use the … verses found
without doubt**. Those passages can then be found in the document; the
doubtful chapters stay readable in the document as they are, and the Bible
stays a document.

#### Finding a passage in a document Bible

Type a passage in **Find**, for example `John 3:16`, or click a reference
anywhere while the document Bible is open:

- if its verses were found, they are shown in the edition's own words, with
  **Show it on page …** to open the document there;
- if you marked where it is (below), the marked place is listed — click it;
- otherwise Abiding Word says plainly **where it is in this document has not
  been identified yet**. It never shows another Bible's words in its place;
  **Read it in …** opens the Bible you read by passage instead.

The line under the title tells you how much can be found this way.

#### Marking where a passage is

Go to the page (PDF) or paragraph (click it in **Text**) where a passage is,
then choose **Mark a passage here** (or **Mark where it is** from a passage),
type the passage and **Save the mark**. Words you selected first are kept
with it as a check. Only the place is recorded: the document, your notes and
their references stay exactly as they are. Remove a mark from the passage's
list.

This works for scanned PDFs too: their pages can be read and marked, though
their words cannot be searched.

## Searching the Bible

1. In the app bar at the top, make sure the search menu says **Scripture**.
2. Type words, for example `living water`, and press **Enter**.
3. Click a result to open that verse.

Put words in quotation marks, like `"living water"`, to find that exact phrase.

> [!TIP]
> Want **every** place a single word appears, sorted by book? Use the
> [Concordance](07-concordance.md) instead.
