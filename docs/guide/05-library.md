# 📚 5. Your library

[← Studies](04-studies.md) · [Contents](README.md) · Next: [Bible references in your books →](06-bible-references.md)

---

Your **library** holds your own books, articles, sermon notes and documents.
Abiding Word reads them, notices every Bible passage they mention, and shows
the right ones beside the passage you are studying.

> [!NOTE]
> **Private by default.** Your library stays on your computer. Abiding Word
> keeps its own copy of each file and **never changes your original**.

## What you can add

| File | Notes |
|---|---|
| **PDF** | Books and articles with real text (most PDFs). *Scanned PDFs* (photos of pages) can't be read yet. |
| **EPUB** | E-books |
| **Word** (`.docx`) | Documents, sermon manuscripts |
| **Markdown** (`.md`) and **text** (`.txt`) | Plain notes |

## Adding a book or document

1. Click **Add to library…** (at the bottom of the Sources pane, or on the
   Library page).
2. Click **Choose a file…** and pick the file.
3. Abiding Word reads it and shows a short summary first:

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/add-source.png" alt="The 'Add to library' summary: PDF, 3 pages, text extracted successfully, 1 Bible reference detected, with title, author, language, kind and sharing fields" width="900">
</p>

4. Check the **title**, **author** and **language**. The language matters:
   it decides how Bible references are read (a German book writes
   *Joh 3,16*, an English one *John 3:16*).
5. Choose the **kind** (book, article, notes, sermon, lecture, document) and
   whether it may be shared. *My own copy — not to be shared* is the safe
   choice for books you bought.
6. Click **Add to library**.

## Books for the passage you are reading

The **Sources** pane on the right always lists the books in your library that
deal with the passage open on the left, and says **why**:

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/sources.png" alt="The Sources pane: 'Sources for John 15:1–11' with four books and why each one is listed" width="440">
</p>

| It says | It means |
|---|---|
| **Mentions John 15:5** | The book quotes or refers to that verse |
| **Section on John 15:1–11** | A heading in the book names the passage, so the whole section below it is about it |
| **p. 12** or **¶ 1** | Where in the book: page 12, or the first paragraph |

Books whose headings are about your passage come first. Click a book to read
it right there. Click the small extra lines to jump to other places in the
same book.

> [!NOTE]
> Abiding Word only lists what a book **actually says**. It does not guess
> what "might be related".

## Reading a book

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/source-reading.png" alt="'Notes on Abiding' open in the Sources pane, with the section 'John 15:1–11 — The True Vine' highlighted and Bible references underlined" width="440">
</p>

- **Back to Sources** returns to the list.
- **Open full reader** gives the book the whole window. Your place is kept.
- The **↶ ↷** arrows go back and forward through the places you visited in
  the book.
- **Find** looks for a word inside this book (see below).
- **Contents** jumps to a chapter; **Marks** lists your marks in the book.
- 🔖 and 📝 bookmark the place you are reading or add a note there (see
  [Marking your books](#marking-your-books)).
- Underlined Bible references can be opened in the Bible. See
  [Bible references in your books](06-bible-references.md).

### Finding a word in this book

Click **Find** (or press **Ctrl+F**) and type a word.

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/find-in-source.png" alt="Find in 'Notes on Abiding': the word 'remain' typed, '1 of 2', and both places marked in the text" width="440">
</p>

- Every place the word appears is marked, and the counter says where you are,
  for example **1 of 2**.
- Press **Enter** for the next place, **Shift+Enter** for the previous one, or
  use the small arrows.
- Capital letters and accents don't matter: *rom* also finds *Röm*.
- Press **Esc** (or **✕**) to close. Your place in the book stays where it is.

This searches **only the book you have open**. To search all your books at
once, use the search box in the app bar (see below).

### Contents

**Contents** lists the book's chapters. Click one to go there; the **↶**
arrow brings you back. Subsections are folded away so long lists stay short:
click the small **›** arrow beside a chapter to see its subsections. When you
open Contents, the chapter you are reading is already unfolded.

Some books have no table of contents of their own. Abiding Word then makes
one from the book's headings and calls it **Generated contents**, so you
always know it was not part of the original book. If the headings are not
clear enough, it shows none rather than a wrong one.

### Reading or Original?

PDF, EPUB and Word files have a **Reading | Original** switch:

| View | What you see | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reading** | The text, cleanly laid out, with clickable Bible references | Reading and studying |
| **Original** | The real pages, exactly as the file looks: pictures, columns, footnotes | Checking the original layout |

In the **Original** view of a **PDF**, Bible references on the page are
underlined too. Click one and choose **Open in Bible**:

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/pdf-original.png" alt="A PDF page in the Original view: the reference 'John 15:4' underlined, with 'Open in Bible' below it" width="900">
</p>

- **Find** works here too: it opens the right page and marks the word when it
  can tell exactly which one it is.
- A page that is only a picture (a scanned page) says **"Native text
  unavailable on this page"**. Its words can't be read or clicked.
- Abiding Word remembers whether you last used **Reading** or **Original** for
  each book, and opens it that way next time.

> [!NOTE]
> This part of Abiding Word is new and still being tested. Bible references in
> page headers and footers, and in sideways or right-to-left text, are left
> unclickable on purpose.

## Marking your books

Mark your books the way you would mark a printed book: highlight a sentence,
write a note in the margin, put a bookmark in a page. Your marks are kept by
Abiding Word, never written into your file.

| Mark | How |
|---|---|
| 🖍️ **Highlight** | Select the words, then choose **Highlight** and a colour |
| 📝 **Note** | Select the words and choose **Add note**, or use the 📝 button for a note on the paragraph you are reading |
| 🔖 **Bookmark** | The 🔖 button marks the place you are reading. Press it again to remove the bookmark |

When you select words, a small bar appears at the bottom of the reader:

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/source-selection.png" alt="Words selected in 'Notes on Abiding', with a small bar at the bottom: Selected text, Add note, Highlight" width="440">
</p>

Highlighted words are coloured; words with a note are underlined with dots. A
small symbol at the end of a paragraph shows that it has marks. Click it to
see them, change a highlight's colour, edit or delete a note, or remove a
bookmark:

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/source-marks.png" alt="A highlighted phrase and a note in 'Notes on Abiding', with the paragraph's marks open: the note and Edit note, Delete note" width="440">
</p>

- The five colours are the same as in the Bible. No colour has a fixed meaning.
- A selection may run over several paragraphs.
- **Without a mouse:** the 📝 button writes a note on the paragraph you are
  at, and offers **Highlight this paragraph**.

### Marks in the Original view

Your marks are shown in the **Original** view too, on the real page, at any
zoom:

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/pdf-marks.png" alt="A PDF page in the Original view with a phrase highlighted across two lines" width="900">
</p>

- In a **PDF** you can also select words on the page and mark them: the same
  small bar appears below the page. The 🔖 and 📝 buttons mark the page you
  are looking at, even a scanned page.

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/pdf-selection.png" alt="Words selected on a PDF page in the Original view, with the bar below the page: Selected text 'first readers before asking', Add note, Highlight" width="900">
</p>

- In **EPUB** and **Word** documents your marks are shown on the page; to make
  a new mark there, select the words in **Reading**.
- Abiding Word only draws a mark where it finds exactly the same words. If it
  can't be sure (a page header, an unusual page), it doesn't guess. Select
  the words in **Reading** instead.

### All your marks in a book

**Marks**, next to **Contents**, lists every mark in the book in reading
order. Click one to go there. To see the marks from all your books and the
Bible together, use **Marks** in the Scripture pane (see
[Highlights, notes and bookmarks](03-highlights-notes-bookmarks.md#finding-your-marks-again-marks)).

> [!NOTE]
> If the text of a book changes (for example after Abiding Word repairs how
> an e-book was read), your marks follow their words. If a mark's words can no
> longer be found for certain, it is kept with its words and says **"This place
> can no longer be found in the text."** It is never moved to other words.

## Using a book in your study

When something you read belongs in your study, bring it there in one step,
without losing where it came from.

1. **Select the words** (or open a highlight or note you made earlier).
2. Click **Add to Study**.
3. A small panel shows the words and where they come from. Abiding Word
   suggests the study you have open and the part you last worked in; change
   either if you like.
4. Write **your own note** about it if you want to. Left empty, it is added as
   a **Quotation**; once you write, it becomes an **Observation**. Pick another
   kind if it fits better.
5. Click **Add to Study** (or press **Ctrl + Enter**). **Esc** or **Cancel**
   adds nothing.

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/add-to-study.png" alt="The Add to Study panel in the reader: the selected words with their author, title and section, a field for your own note, the kind, and the part of the study" width="440">
</p>

- It works on words you select in **Reading** and on the pages of a **PDF**
  in the **Original** view, with no highlight needed first.
- A **highlight** or a **note** can be added from its paragraph's marks, from
  the book's **Marks** list, or from **Marks** in the Scripture pane. A note
  you wrote while reading starts as your note in the study; the book's words
  stay with it.
- Bookmarks only mark a place, so they have nothing to add.
- Nothing is ever added to a study by itself. A highlight stays a highlight
  until you choose **Add to Study**.

In the study, the note shows **your words first**, the book's words quietly
under them, and where they come from. Click the source to open the book beside
your study at those very words.

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/source-in-study.png" alt="A study note: the person's own words, the quoted words from the book in italics, and the citation 'Study group notes, Notes on Abiding, The True Vine' as a link" width="640">
</p>

> [!NOTE]
> Your study keeps the quotation and where it came from, even if you later
> remove the book from your library or delete the highlight. The book can no
> longer be opened then, but nothing you added is lost.

### Copying a quotation with its source

Choose **⋯ More actions → Copy quotation with citation** on selected words, a
highlight, a note with words, or a quotation in your study. You get something
you can paste anywhere:

```
“As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself”
— J. Example, Notes on Abiding, p. 42
```

Only what Abiding Word knows about the book is used: if there is no author, it
is left out; if there is no page number, the chapter or section is given
instead.

## Searching your library

1. In the app bar, switch the search menu to **Library**.
2. Type a word and press **Enter**.

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/search.png" alt="Library search for 'remain' with three results, each showing the book and exactly where the passage is" width="900">
</p>

Every result says exactly where it comes from: the book, the section or page.
Click a result to open the book at that place.

## Citing a book in a study note

The quickest way is **Add to Study** while you read (see
[Using a book in your study](#using-a-book-in-your-study)). You can also cite
a book from the study itself: in a
[study note](04-studies.md#study-notes-capturing-what-you-discover), open
**Details** and search your library for the passage you want to cite, then
click **Cite**. The citation stays readable even if you later remove the book
from your library.

## Removing a book

On the Library page, choose **Move to Trash…** beside the book. It leaves your
library, the Sources pane and search at once, and **Undo** brings it straight
back. From Trash (Settings → Sources) you can restore it for 30 days; after
that it is deleted for good. While it is in Trash your marks in it are hidden,
not deleted, and come back with it.

Deleting permanently is done from Trash. Citations in your studies keep the
title and author, so your notes still make sense. Only Abiding Word's own copy
of the file is deleted — **your original file is never touched**.

## Looking after your library (Settings → Sources)

**Settings → Sources** lists every book and document you have added:

- **Find** by title, author or file name; **Filters** narrow the list by
  category, language, file type, collection, tag, scanned files, or files
  added more than once; **Sort by** title, author, category, date added,
  language or status.
- Click a source to see its file, how its text was read and any duplicates,
  and to correct its **details** (title, author, category, year, publisher,
  licence …). Its text and your marks are not changed.
- **Collections** are your own folders, and can sit inside each other. A
  source can be in several; its file is never copied. **Tags** work the same
  way, and many can go on one source.
- Tick several sources (or **Select all on this page**) to **Archive**,
  **Move to Trash…**, add them to a collection or tag them all at once.
- **Archived** sources are kept but stay out of your everyday lists; show them
  with **Show → Archived** and **Restore** them any time.
- In **Trash**, **Restore** or **Delete permanently**. Deleting several at
  once asks you to type their number first, so it cannot happen by accident.

Every confirmation starts on **Cancel**: pressing Enter, Escape or clicking
outside it never deletes anything.
